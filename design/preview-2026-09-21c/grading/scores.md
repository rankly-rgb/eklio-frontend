These scores come from an independent pass that saw only the eleven reference compositions in `design/reference/posts/` and the thirty produced visuals in `design/preview-2026-09-21c/visuals/` — no source code, no build output, no knowledge of how the images were produced.

# Grading — 30 post visuals, 2026-09-21c

Criteria, each 1–5:

1. **illustration** — is a drawn object present, and does it carry the meaning of what the card says?
2. **tints** — does each part of a multi-part diagram get its own tint, and are the tints softened flats rather than saturated brand colour?
3. **legibility** — is every piece of text comfortably readable, with clear hierarchy and no crowding or collision?
4. **elaboration** — against the reference of the same composition type, same level of work, or thinner?

Note on the lone-sentence cards: their specified form is text plus a small drawn mark and no tinted field. They are scored against that, so they take 4 on illustration and 4 on tints by type-correctness. Eleven of thirty cards are lone sentences, which lifts those two means; read the diagram-card scores separately before treating the averages as healthy.

## The three changes that would most raise the overall mean

**1. Soften the tints, and give every part of a diagram its own one.** This is the single largest and most systematic deduction: it touches all nineteen diagram cards. The reference tints are pale flats — a warm off-white, a soft oat, a dusty blush, a muted sage — all sitting close to the paper so the ink reads at full strength. What is produced is the brand ochre and the brand terracotta at something near full saturation, so most diagram cards carry one or two heavy blocks against a pale ground, ink contrast inside them drops, and the composition reads as banded rather than tinted. Fix the ramp and then fix the assignment: 19 and 22 repeat the first tint on their fourth field, and 16 gives four fields only three tints. Expected lift: roughly +1.0 to +1.3 on tints, with a knock-on gain on legibility.

**2. Draw the illustration from the card's own content, not from its composition type.** Every surface card gets the same blob, every numbered card the same potted plant, every cycle the same loop, both comparison cards the same pair of empty boxes. The illustration is currently a property of the template, so it cannot carry the meaning of any particular card — a plant sits over "loss of identity, loss of job" and over "grief rewires before thought" without changing. The comparison bubbles are the worst case: empty containers that would read identically with any content in the fields. The quadrant head on 16 shows what the set can do when the drawing is chosen for the idea. Expected lift: roughly +0.8 on illustration and +0.7 on elaboration, since the two scores move together.

**3. Restore the label-plus-gloss pair on every field, and stop the text from overrunning its box.** Cards 10, 13, 19 and 22 carry bare labels where the reference pairs a 1–3 word label with a short gloss underneath in every field — half the content, and it is why those four sit at the bottom of the table. Alongside it, fix the text-fitting failures the same pass would catch: the truncated title on 24, the title/label collision on 25, the three-line gloss wraps and mid-phrase label breaks also on 25, and the over-budget lone sentences on 12 (17 words), 20 (16) and 29 (15). Expected lift: roughly +0.4 on legibility and +0.4 on elaboration.

A fourth change worth naming, though smaller: the set spends eleven of thirty cards on lone sentences and leaves three reference compositions — lettered-technique, concentric-control, practitioner-card — entirely unused, while the three cards labelled carousel are single panels with a fraction badge rather than the three-panel form the reference shows. Broadening the mix would raise elaboration without touching the renderer.
